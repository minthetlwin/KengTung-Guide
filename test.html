Sacred Chronology & Seven Centuries of Relic Protection

The foundation of Wat Zom Kham is deeply woven into the spiritual genealogy of the Keng Tung valley, from the early blessing of wandering Arhat monks to the high golden age of the hereditary Saopha rulers.

1253 CE Founding by King Mangrai’s Successors

Establishment upon Zom Kham Knoll

Following the mythic taming of the ancient lake waters by the holy hermit Maha Kassapa and disciples, the third ruler of the Mangrai dynastic branch decreed that a grand gilded chedi be erected atop the prominent central hill overlooking Lake Naung Tung to serve as the spiritual core of the Tai Khün people.

Annals Ref: Chronicle of Jengtung, Palm Leaf MSS-12
Archive Scan
1780 CE Saopha Royal Patronage & Relic Enshrinement

The Sacred Gautama Hair Relics Deposited

The Saopha (Celestial Prince) of Keng Tung ordered a complete structural casing of the primary stupa, increasing its circumference to 180 cubits and depositing six sacred strands of the Buddha's hair into an inner jeweled chamber alongside royal silver seals, amber votives, and ruby reliquaries.

Custodial Note: Reliquary remains hermetically sealed beneath the octagonal plinth
Relic Archive
1924 CE Colonial Era Elevation

Raising of the 9-Tiered Gemstone Parasol (Hti)

A community-wide metallurgical endeavor synthesized 45 kilograms of brass, pure gold leaf, and 1,200 dangling gemstone bells. When the wind sweeps across Zom Kham Hill, these wind-chimes reverberate sacred frequencies tuned to classical Tai Khün devotional modes.

Restoration Master: Master Goldsmith U Sai Hlaing of Keng Tung Guild

Present Era Perpetual Trustee Stewardship

Continuous Gilding & Scientific Conservation

Governed under the oversight of the Wat Zom Kham Gopaka Trustee Committee, the stupa receives regular artisanal re-gilding with pure Shan gold leaf pounded in the traditional Loi Mwe workshops, ensuring zero structural degradation through monsoon cycles.

Annual Sacred Rituals & Pilgrim Protocol

The ritual calendar follows the lunar astrological movements of the Tai Khün calendar. Participation is welcomed for respectful observers adhering to traditional Theravada decorum.

Full Moon of Kason (May) Astrological Cycle: Lunar VI

Sacred Full Moon Bathing Ritual (Buddha Bathing)

Commemorating the Birth, Enlightenment, and Parinirvana of Gautama Buddha. Long bamboo chutes and pulleys carry scented sandal and jasmine water to the mid-tier rings of the golden stupa spire, poured by monastic elders and hereditary chieftains.

Merit-Making Procedure
  • check_circle Assemble at southern staircase courtyard prior to 06:30 AM with water vessels.
  • check_circle Participate in communal five precepts chant led by Wat Zom Kham Sayadaw.
  • check_circle Circumambulate the chedi clockwise (Pradaksina) three times in silent meditation.
Next Observance: May 12, 2025 Public Participation Permitted
Full Moon of Thadingyut (October) Astrological Cycle: Lunar XI

Thadingyut Floating Lantern & Candlelight Homage

Marking the Buddha's descent from Tavatimsa heaven. The entire circumference of Zom Kham terrace is illuminated by over 5,000 earthenware oil lamps, while hot-air paper fire-balloons (Khmu Phay) are released above the hill into the night sky.

Ascent Wayfinding: Naung Tung Lake to the Summit

Wat Zom Kham presides over the central hill in Kyaing Tong. Access is available via ceremonial covered staircases or the southern vehicular switchback.

directions_walk Walking Ascent from Naung Tung Lake

Ceremonial Eastern Dragon Stairway

Begins at the northeastern lakeside promenade. Guarded by twin 18th-century stucco Chinthes (mythical lions). Shaded by ancient Bodhi trees with rest pavilions at step 80 and step 150. Duration: approx. 12-18 minutes leisurely stroll.

local_taxi Vehicular & Accessible Access

South Crest Drive & Wheelchair Ramps

Taxis, tuk-tuks, and motorbikes can navigate the paved winding ridge road directly to the Upper South Gate. Level ramp access directly connects the upper car park to the stupa's primary marble pradaksina ambulatory.
